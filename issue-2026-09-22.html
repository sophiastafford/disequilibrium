Keeping up with AI & the Entry-Level Job Market | September 22, 2026

What Changed This Week

At companies that adopted AI last year, total headcount went up. A working paper covering 41 countries, 1.25 billion job postings and 154 million employment records found employment at those firms grew 3.3%, with senior roles up 6.7% and junior roles down about 3%. The AI job market is growing and hiring, but almost all of that growth sits in senior roles, so the entry-level problem is a missing bottom rung rather than weak demand. That distinction changes what you do about it. Weak demand means waiting for the cycle to turn. A missing rung means the job you want may never be posted at the level you are applying to. The damage shows up as a slower search rather than no job. A new National Bureau of Economic Research paper finds job-finding and job-switching rates falling for AI-exposed workers, and the natural rate of unemployment up roughly 0.1 to 0.2 points since large language models arrived. The rest of this issue is about which employers are hiring upward, the one industry where the ladder held, and the two states that will now tell you why an algorithm rejected you.

Tech

Two research teams measured the same market from opposite ends this week and both found the junior share of hiring collapsing while total employment rose. Bharat Chandar of Stanford and Bouke Klein Teeselink of King's College London found senior employment at AI-adopting firms up 6.7% while junior employment fell about 3%. Indeed Hiring Lab found advertised pay in the most AI-exposed occupations up about 46% since 2021, but that premium disappears once you control for seniority, because entry-level postings in those fields fell from 29% of the total to 10% while senior postings rose from 22% to 47%. If an AI wage premium looks like a reason to enter a field, check the seniority mix of its openings first. (Bloomberg | Insurance Journal | Indeed Hiring Lab)

Oracle told investors in writing that AI adoption has resulted in reductions to its workforce, after cutting about 21,000 jobs in fiscal 2026. The disclosure sits in the company's annual report, and another round began on September 14. PayPal cut 251 headquarters jobs and said it expects a further 20% reduction over the next few years in order to invest more in AI. KPMG trimmed about 4% of its Advisory division, and the cuts landed on its AI, cyber, SAP and testing teams. For students targeting consulting, an AI practice group is not automatically the safe place to land, so ask whether that team sells billable client delivery or internal capability. (Martin Cid Magazine | Crunchbase News | The Register)

Ethan Mollick argues the models already have far more capability than anyone is using, and names four things they still lack. Deep domain expertise, wide interdisciplinary knowledge, taste and agency. He cites Anthropic research indicating that expertise shapes the quality of what a model returns, so experts get better output from the same system. As generation gets cheap, curation becomes the scarce skill. For students deciding how to spend the next two years, going deep enough in one subject to know when a model is wrong about it beats collecting another tool on your resume. (One Useful Thing)

Law

Demand for large-firm attorney hours grew 4.2% in the first half of 2026 against a historic norm near 1.5%, with revenue up 11.7%. Thomson Reuters and Bloomberg Law data show AI behaving as a complement to billable hours rather than a substitute, because cheaper legal work per unit is inducing clients to buy more of it. Law is the counterexample to this week's other numbers. For pre-law students, the question to put to a firm is whether that growth is being staffed with junior associates or with contract and lateral hires. (TaxProf Blog | Thomson Reuters Institute)

Latitude Legal, which grew new clients 52% in the first half of 2026, staffs almost entirely former law firm partners and senior in-house counsel. The flexible-talent provider named Alex Su president this week, and his argument is that as AI improves, demand rises for lawyers with the judgment to direct it and own the result. The fastest-growing part of the legal market never touches the junior rung. For law students, that makes a first job whose purpose is supervised reps of real client work the thing worth optimizing for. (LawNext)

Among 486 summer associates surveyed this year, 96% used AI during their program, up from 80% a year earlier, and 9% of firms now required it. Law360 Pulse found 97% using it for legal research and only 1% of firms discouraging it. Meanwhile at least 12 law schools rewrote their AI policies over the summer, and about a quarter now ban it outright. For rising 2Ls, AI use is the floor in a summer program, so what distinguishes you is being able to say why you rejected an AI answer. (Law360 Pulse)

Government & Policy

California's legislature passed four workplace AI bills that become law automatically if the governor does not act by September 30, and none of them covers hiring. SB 947 requires human review and written notice when discipline or termination relies primarily on an automated system. SB 951 expands the state's layoff-notice law so mass-layoff notices must name the job functions being automated. AB 1883 bans emotion recognition and neural-data collection on employees. Candidate screening, the front door of the pipeline, is untouched. For applicants, assume no right to an explanation when an algorithm screens you out in California. (National Law Review)

States filed 155 bills on AI in the workplace this year and enacted two. MultiState's session review found Colorado's SB 189 and Connecticut's SB 5 are the only ones that became law, and both require employers using automated hiring tools to notify applicants, explain an adverse outcome in detail, allow a request for human review, and correct inaccurate personal data. Applicant-notification bills died in Minnesota, Iowa and New York. If you are applying to employers in Colorado or Connecticut, ask in writing for that explanation and that review after a screening-stage rejection, because almost nowhere else can you. (MultiState)
