## What Changed This Week

A law firm partner reviews a draft written by AI and finds it as good as what he would expect from a first- or second-year associate. That scene sums up the week. AI is taking over the beginner work that new graduates used to learn on, even as AI layoffs fade.

Both halves show up in the data. Challenger, Gray & Christmas, an outplacement firm that tracks announced job cuts, counted 1,265 cuts blamed on AI in October, the lowest month of 2026. Employers added 165,000 jobs and unemployment slipped to 4.1%. But the hires rate is stuck at 3.4%. When AI writes the first draft of a memo or a block of code, a company has less reason to hire a junior and train them on it. The loss shows up as missing practice, not as a pink slip.

That also creates an opening. Checking whether AI output is right is still human work, and it is a skill juniors can build. The University of Chicago is betting on it, requiring every first-year student to take an AI-free writing course starting next fall. The rest of this issue is about where the beginner work is going and how to find a job that still gives you supervised practice.

## Tech

**AI was cited for 1,265 job cuts in October, the lowest monthly total of 2026, and tech posted its smallest month of cuts this year at 5,576.** Total announced cuts fell to 43,994, down 71% from a year earlier. Hiring plans jumped to 444,692, but almost all of that is Amazon's record 300,000 seasonal hires. Tech still leads 2026 with 171,501 cuts, and AI has been named in about 20% of all cuts this year. For CS majors, the layoff wave is cooling, but a surge built on warehouse jobs does nothing for a December graduate looking for a first salaried role. Read the headline numbers by job type before deciding the market has turned. ([Challenger, Gray & Christmas](https://www.challengergray.com/blog/october-layoffs-at-three-year-low-seasonal-hiring-plans-up-26/) | [Challenger Report](https://www.challengergray.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Challenger-Report-October-2026.pdf))

**Employers hired at a 3.4% rate in September while laying off just 1.0%, which keeps the labor market frozen in place.** The Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) reported 7.5 million job openings, 5.4 million hires and 1.6 million layoffs and discharges, all little changed. Quits rose by 375,000 over the year. In a market this slow, people already inside are fairly safe and people trying to get in wait longer. Openings exist, but fewer of them turn into offers. That lack of hiring hurts new graduates more than layoffs do, because a first job depends on someone deciding to bring you in. If you're graduating this year, referrals and internship return offers convert to jobs more reliably than a high volume of cold applications. ([Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/jolts.nr0.htm))

## Law

**A law firm partner says AI drafts now rival the work of first- and second-year associates, and he worries firms will stop training juniors as a result.** Writing in Above the Law, a partner at Goldberg Segalla warns that firms may use AI to absorb more work with the lawyers they already have, or hire experienced laterals who need less supervision. Either way, new lawyers get fewer repetitions. His fix is supervised client work for juniors, with responsibility for checking AI output before anyone relies on it. The same week, Christa Acampora, dean of arts and sciences at the University of Virginia (UVA), said that AI is increasingly capable of doing what students should be developing for that first job. Colleges cannot predict what employers will need four years out, she argued, so they should build judgment and curiosity. For 1Ls and 2Ls, the useful pitch to a firm is no longer that you can produce a draft. It is that you can catch what the draft got wrong. Look for clinics and externships that give you real review responsibility. ([Above the Law](https://abovethelaw.com/2026/11/making-yourself-ai-proof/) | [Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/student-success/life-after-college/2026/11/05/what-purpose-college-age-ai))
