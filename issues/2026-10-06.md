## What Changed This Week

This week, California’s governor signed three laws about AI and layoffs one day after UCLA researchers reported that they could not find AI in the unemployment data for young graduates. Both facts point the same direction. AI’s damage to new-grad jobs has not clearly shown up in the data yet, but California, employers and economists are already acting as if it will. Unemployment counts who is out of work today. Lawmakers and economists are reacting to a different set of numbers, such as entry-level postings that Harvard scholars say have fallen 73% over four years. That gap explains why a weak jobs report and a no-effect study landed in the same week. The caution is real, since the UCLA authors call their result early. So is the opening, because employers that are not training anyone to use these tools will pay attention to graduates who already can. The rest of this issue covers where the rules and the data diverge, and what that lets you ask an employer before you sign.

## Government & Policy

**California’s governor signed three laws on September 30 that limit how employers can use AI on workers.** They ban using biometric data to infer a worker’s emotional state, require written notice to employees when AI is responsible for mass layoffs, and bar employers from relying solely on AI to decide firings. Governor Gavin Newsom said AI should expand opportunity rather than come at the expense of workers, and he noted that the federal government has no comprehensive AI rules. If you are considering a California employer, these laws set a floor on how AI can be used in layoffs and firings. It is fair to ask employers elsewhere how they handle the same decisions. ([News4JAX](https://www.news4jax.com/news/politics/2026/09/30/california-gov-gavin-newsom-signs-laws-to-protect-workers-from-ai-risks/))

**A UCLA working paper found no significant evidence that AI is raising unemployment among young graduates, even though the rate for ages 22 to 25 reached 7.3%.** That rate is higher than last year but inside the 6.3% to 7.8% range seen since 2022. The researchers found no AI-linked rise in customer service or programming, two heavily exposed fields, and said the finding reflects an early stage. Harvard economists add that only about a third of company AI experiments succeed and that firms are not formally training staff, while Doug Elmendorf warns that serious disruption could still arrive within five to 15 years. For any major, that lack of training suggests that a new hire who has already used these tools well starts ahead. ([University Herald](https://www.universityherald.com/articles/80457/20260929/ai-has-yet-cause-spike-unemployment-among-young-graduates.htm) | [Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2026/09/why-ai-hasnt-triggered-mass-layoffs-yet/))

## Finance & Consulting

**The U.S.** added only 29,000 jobs in September, and financial activities lost another 7,000. Forecasters had expected about 84,000 new jobs. July and August were revised down by a combined 60,000, unemployment rose to 4.2%, and finance has lost 129,000 jobs since May 2025. Health care added 17,000 jobs. The data do not say why finance is shrinking, so they do not show AI as the cause. If you are recruiting for finance or consulting, a sector that has shed jobs for months suggests that fall recruiting will be competitive, and health care is the steadier source of new hires. ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/articles/september-2026-jobs-report-payrolls-123334753.html) | [Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.nr0.htm))
