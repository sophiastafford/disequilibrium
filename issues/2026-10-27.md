## What Changed This Week

Barclays plans to cut about 5,000 jobs this year, but its chief executive says AI is not the main driver. Instead, the cuts are concentrated in support and management roles, while the company is redesigning entry-level positions around AI skills. About 60% of Barclays’ cuts are in support functions, and American Express is similarly eliminating management layers and increasing the number of employees reporting to each manager. Neither company announced cuts to graduate hiring. That distinction matters: the jobs being eliminated are not necessarily the entry-level positions students have been warned are most vulnerable to AI. Instead, companies appear to be consolidating support functions and flattening management structures. Meanwhile, Weil, a major law firm, is investing in new talent through a summer program that teaches first-year law students to code and build AI systems.

This issue explores where companies are cutting jobs, where they are investing in new hires, and what that means for students entering the workforce.

## Finance & Consulting

**Barclays will cut about 5,000 jobs, roughly 5% of its workforce, and its chief executive says AI is not the main driver.** The bank has already removed 1,000 positions and expects about 4,000 more by the end of 2026, as part of a plan to save £1.1 billion in 2027. About 60% of the actions are in support functions, mostly operations in UK retail banking, with some investment bank roles also affected. CEO C.S. Venkatakrishnan called AI “the icing on the cake” and said process simplification has driven most of the efficiency so far. Coverage reads the cuts as an AI story anyway, which suggests the function being cut matters more than the label. If you are weighing a bank offer, ask whether the role sits in front-office work or in operations the bank is simplifying. ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/22/barclays-ai-jobs-cuts.html) | [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/barclays-to-cut-5-000-jobs-while-handing-work-over-to-ai-chief-executive-says-ai-is-icing-on-the-cake))

**American Express will cut about 5% of its workforce to prepare for AI, and it is removing management layers as part of that.** On its October 23 earnings call, the company said roughly 3,500 to 3,800 jobs would go, with outlets reporting different counts, and booked a $336 million restructuring charge. It is widening spans of control, meaning each manager oversees more people, and its chief financial officer described the cuts as preparation to automate later. CEO Stephen Squeri said AI agents will only get access to company systems through approved channels. Fewer management layers likely means fewer steps between a new hire and senior work. For finance majors, that points to broader scope earlier, and possibly less formal supervision to learn from. ([HR Reporter](https://www.hrreporter.com/news/hr-news/amex-to-cut-5-per-cent-of-workforce-to-prepare-for-ai/395071) | [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-23/amex-raises-guidance-after-earnings-beat-wall-street-estimates))

## Law

**Weil, a large law firm, will teach selected first-year law students to build AI tools and offer them a return spot in 2028.** The 2027 summer program runs 10 weeks with the firm’s LASER team, short for Lawyer Augmented Solutions Engineering and Research. Participants work on active legal projects, learn Python from scratch, build a neural network, and help develop legal software under a dedicated mentor. Those selected receive offers to return for Weil’s second-year summer program in 2028. Chief AI & Innovation Officer Andrew Simon said the goal is not to turn them into software engineers. If you are a 1L, ask firms whether they train people to build with AI or only to use it, since Weil treats technical understanding as part of the path in. ([Artificial Lawyer](https://www.artificiallawyer.com/2026/10/21/weil-starts-special-ai-program-for-summer-associates/))
