## What Changed This Week

At Anthropic, the share of technical hires who were junior fell from 27% to 9%. AI's effect on new grads is now showing up in the data, as jobs that never get posted rather than as layoffs. That difference explains why this summer's unemployment numbers looked calm. Unemployment counts people who lose work or can't find it, but it can't see a role that was never opened. The Federal Reserve's Beige Book, a survey of business contacts nationwide, found the same pattern this week. Firms in five of twelve regional districts expect less job creation after AI productivity gains, and they are shrinking through attrition and delayed hiring rather than cuts.

The other half of the week's data points the opposite way. AI is creating human work, from 1,000 contractors patching code at Bank of America to doctors and lawyers paid to train models. That work mostly arrives as contracts, and it pays for expertise you can start building now. The rest of this issue follows where openings are closing, where new work is appearing, and how to tell the two apart when you job-hunt.

## Tech

**Anthropic, the company behind Claude, reports that the junior share of its technical hires fell from 27% to 9%.** In a study published October 16, its economics team found that the junior share of technical staff dropped from 21% to 14% since early 2025, and junior postings fell sharply after December 2025. By August, AI agent hours ran about six times the hours of the employees directing them. The report concedes that its redeployment programs do nothing for people who were never hired. For CS majors, the hiring managers in the study now weight autonomy, judgment, and creative AI use above specific technical skills, so a record of independent, AI-directed projects carries more weight than coursework alone. ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/employment-at-anthropic) | [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-has-nearly-stopped-hiring-junior-staff-because-ai-agents-now-work-six-times-as-many-hours/))

**Junior job postings now number about one for every four senior postings, down from one per 2.5 in 2022.** Lightcast, a labor-market analytics firm, reported on October 19 that the U.S. share of postings mentioning AI skills rose 60% in the first half of 2026. That demand sits at the top of the ladder. Only 1.4% of junior postings mention AI skills, compared with 9.3% of senior ones. If you're applying to entry-level roles, the gap suggests that demonstrated AI skill is still unusual at your level, which makes it a way to stand out from applicants who simply match the listing. ([Lightcast](https://lightcast.io/resources/blog/tracking-ai-workforce-trends-october-2026-update))

## Finance & Consulting

**Bank of America hired about 1,000 contractors to fix software flaws that an AI model found.** CEO Brian Moynihan told CNBC on October 14 that the bank added the contractors after Anthropic's Mythos model surfaced vulnerabilities, at a cost of "a couple hundred million plus." He called the spending temporary. The same week, Bloomberg reported that doctors, lawyers, and engineers are training AI models for $80 to $450 an hour, and that Mercor, a platform that hires experts for this work, has about 30,000 active contractors. AI is creating human work, but it mostly comes as contracts. For finance and cybersecurity students, these roles can build skills quickly, so weigh them as on-ramps rather than as a substitute for a first full-time job. ([TechRadar](https://techradar.com/pro/security/bank-of-america-had-to-hire-1-000-contractors-to-patch-software-flaws-spotted-by-anthropic-mythos) | [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-16/ai-training-jobs-are-attracting-doctors-lawyers-and-engineers))

**Accenture capped company-paid Claude use at $29 per employee per month after some staff spent up to $1,000.** According to 404 Media, the consulting firm's memo sends extra usage to project budget codes and ties access to role and business need. At least one employee lost access entirely. Microsoft, Atlassian, and Uber have also moved to budget employee AI spending. For students recruiting into consulting, AI access at large firms is becoming a rationed resource, so new analysts who can show what their AI use produced for a client will likely get more of it. ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/accenture-claude-29-a-month-cap) | [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/ai-news/accenture-ai-spending-limits/))

## Healthcare

**Early-career physicians submit a disproportionate share of questions to AI clinical tools, according to a study of 270 million queries.** The Harvard Medical School study, published October 15 in the Journal of the American Medical Association (JAMA), tracked about 950,000 U.S. physicians, physician assistants, and nurse practitioners using OpenEvidence, an AI clinical reference tool. About 52% of queries concerned clinical decisions. Three co-authors work for OpenEvidence, and effects on patient outcomes have not been studied. For pre-med and medical students, AI-assisted reasoning is already routine among young doctors, so learning to check these tools against primary sources is now part of clinical training. ([Harvard Medical School](https://hms.harvard.edu/news/first-large-scale-study-shows-how-doctors-use-ai-medicine))

**State medical boards are writing rules for AI that performs clinical work with little or no physician supervision.** STAT reported on October 14 that the Federation of State Medical Boards (FSMB) convened a 22-member workgroup that includes the American Medical Association, the American Hospital Association, Anthropic, and the insurer Elevance Health. Because states license medical practice, its guidance could become a template for state laws, and a draft could be public within weeks to months. If you're heading toward medicine, nursing, or physician assistant programs, these rules will help decide which tasks clinicians are hired to do, so track them alongside residency and job data. ([STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2026/10/14/lobbyists-trade-groups-ama-push-autonomous-clinical-ai-regulations/))
