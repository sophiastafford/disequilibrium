## What Changed This Week

What does a college degree still buy you? Two sets of findings released this week point to a relatively clear answer: graduates are still getting hired, but the degree may not be worth as much as it used to be.

A new National Bureau of Economic Research (NBER) paper found no increase in unemployment among recent college graduates this summer compared with older graduates or young workers without degrees. So far, AI does not seem to be eliminating entry-level jobs for college graduates. But it may be changing what those jobs pay. Four days earlier, 123 economists surveyed by Indeed Hiring Lab made their largest cut yet to expected earnings for college-educated workers, while leaving their expectations for workers without degrees unchanged. The problem is not necessarily whether graduates can find work. It is whether a college degree still gives them the same earnings advantage it once did. Underemployment, which counts graduates working in jobs that do not require a degree, is already at 42%.

The rest of this issue looks at where the value of an entry-level job is being reset, and how to compare opportunities based on what they teach you, what they pay, and where they can lead.

## Tech

**Recent graduates are not losing jobs to AI at a measurable rate, but economists cut their outlook for college-graduate real wages from 47.3 to 42.4.** Robert Fairlie and Jane Wu analyzed Current Population Survey data from June through August and found no increase in relative unemployment for recent college graduates. In Indeed Hiring Lab's survey, where 50 is neutral, the same reading for workers without degrees held at 50.9. For CS majors weighing offers, the credential no longer sets a wage floor, so compare offers on where the pay and the work lead, not the starting number. ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35796) | [Indeed Hiring Lab](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/09/24/q3-labor-market-outlook-survey/))

**Performance marketing agencies say they have stopped hiring college graduates at all.** At a Digiday town hall on September 24, one agency owner said, "We haven't hired college-level grads in some time, and I don't anticipate that we will be." The tasks that once justified a graduate salary, such as building keyword lists, pulling reports and checking placements, no longer carry one. A Stanford-based finding puts the employment loss for workers aged 18 to 34 in AI-exposed occupations at 16%. Here the rung is removed rather than repriced. For marketing and communications majors, an agency with a named graduate training program is a real differentiator ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/agencies-stop-hiring-graduates-as-ai-takes-the-entry-level-work/))

## Finance & Consulting

**The Big Four accounting firms are keeping their graduate intake and changing what graduates do in it.** At KPMG, Deloitte, EY and PwC, AI now handles the data entry and report production that used to fill a trainee's first two years. KPMG front-loads technical and regulatory exams so graduates finish them before client work, and chief people officer Marc Burrows said that graduates will be "more involved in reviewing and exercising judgement rather than simply producing outputs." For accounting and finance students, ask a Big Four recruiter which tasks a first-year owns and when the first judgment call happens, because the firms have publicly committed to moving it earlier. ([City A.M.](https://www.cityam.com/how-the-big-four-are-reshaping-graduate-training-for-an-ai-world/))

**McKinsey, BCG and Bain are all testing AI skills inside the case interview itself.** The three firms are experimenting with letting candidates use AI during cases and with other ways to assess AI capability as part of the screen. MIT Sloan's career office reports that the change has created real anxiety among candidates who prepared for the traditional format. No firm has published a standard format yet. If you are recruiting for consulting this cycle, practice casing with a model in the room and be ready to explain why you accepted or rejected what it told you. ([MIT Sloan Career Development Office](https://cdo.mit.edu/blog/2026/09/23/how-mckinsey-bcg-and-bain-are-testing-ai-skills-in-interviews/))

**Seventy percent of 376 provosts use AI at least weekly, but only 20% say their institution has a coherent vision for AI in teaching.** Inside Higher Ed and Hanover Research surveyed provosts at public and private nonprofit institutions in July and August. Only 10% report a centralized AI strategy. For students recruiting into finance and consulting, the AI fluency these firms now test is something you will likely have to build outside your degree requirements. Of AI skill mentions on resumes, 74% are tied to real projects rather than coursework. ([Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/governance/executive-leadership/2026/09/23/ai-use-funding-cuts-how-provosts-navigate-2026) | [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/25/ai-jobs-recent-graduates-college-degrees.html))

## Law

**Law firms are building in-house AI teams that pay up to $438,000, a second career ladder beside the associate track.** Cooley launched Cooley AI, a firm-owned AI company with 14 open positions, and advertises $220,000 to $250,000 for a senior AI data engineer. One unnamed firm posted up to $438,000 for a director of applied AI. Kirkland & Ellis committed $500 million to a custom AI platform over three to four years, and Morgan & Morgan at least $1 billion over a decade. For law students with any technical background, price this door against the associate track everyone else is lining up for. ([JD Journal](https://www.jdjournal.com/2026/09/25/law-firms-ai-talent/))

**AI companies are recruiting lawyers out of large firms into a new role called legal engineer.** The role pairs legal training with specifying, testing and supervising legal AI systems, and vendors including Harvey advertise it openly. The pull is on qualified lawyers rather than new graduates, so it draws experience out of the firms while they cut the intake that would replace it. For 1Ls, track the posted requirements for legal engineer roles now to see how much practice experience vendors demand before they will hire you. ([Law.com](https://www.law.com/corpcounsel/2026/09/24/ai-companies-recruit-big-law-lawyers-into-new-legal-engineer-career-path-report-finds/))
