Keeping up with AI & the Entry-Level Job Market | September 15, 2026

What Changed This Week

In a study released this week, 133 patent lawyers at eleven firms spent 90 days drafting with an AI tool, and then the researchers took it away and scored their work blind. AI is absorbing the beginner tasks that used to turn a new hire into a competent one, so the thing to judge a first job on now is whether it still teaches you anything. The juniors had gained the most while the tool was running, and kept none of it on average once it was gone. The low-stakes work that used to build judgment is now done by something that does not need to learn. Employers are not pulling back from early-career hiring, though. ManpowerGroup surveyed 39,878 employers this week and found 45% adding more early-career talent than last year against 20% pulling back, and they blamed candidate inexperience more often than AI for the roles they cut. The demand is there. The apprenticeship inside it is coming apart, and the rest of this issue is about where that is happening and what to ask an employer to tell the difference.

Law

Large law firms are on track to hire roughly 2,000 fewer new lawyers this year than in 2024, and two-thirds expect smaller first-year classes in 2028 than in 2025. Bloomberg Law reported the Citi figures, which also put second-year summer classes at their lowest since 2020. No recession is driving this contraction. Firm leaders say they cannot forecast how much junior work AI will absorb. For 1Ls, the 2L summer slot is the bottleneck now, so treat recruiting timelines as fixed and widen your list past the largest firms. (Bloomberg Law | Above the Law)

In that patent-drafting trial, the junior lawyers who gained most from the AI kept none of it once the tool was gone. David Autor and co-authors ran the 90-day experiment with 133 attorneys, with blinded evaluators scoring enforceability, accuracy, completeness and clarity. Junior scores bifurcated rather than rising, producing fewer mediocre drafts but more poor ones and more good ones. Senior attorneys kept their gains. It is the first causal evidence that AI-assisted junior work builds expertise or hollows it out depending on how the workflow is arranged. That turns a vague worry into one callback question. Does a junior here write a first draft before touching the tool? (Patently-O | NBER)

Across 109 UK firms, training contract places fell by 44 seats, with Linklaters cutting its intake from 100 to 60 and Freshfields from 85 to 70. Legal Cheek attributes the largest cuts primarily to firms expanding solicitor apprenticeships rather than to AI. The training is being moved to a different entrance rather than eliminated. For students targeting UK law, check which of your firms are growing apprenticeship intakes, because that may now be the higher-probability door into the same building. (Legal Cheek)

Tech

Anthropic says Claude now writes about 80% of its code and that each of its engineers ships roughly eight times what one shipped from 2021 to 2025. The company stated that writing code is no longer the constraint. IEEE Spectrum reported the same shift elsewhere, with AI writing 42% of code added to shared codebases while 96% of developers say they do not fully trust it, and found firms rebuilding junior onboarding around review. IBM now gives juniors harder projects sooner and has them troubleshoot the failures. For computer science majors, a story about a plausible-looking AI bug you caught is worth more than another finished project. (Anthropic | IEEE Spectrum)

Entry-level postings in computer and mathematical occupations in New York City are down 49% since 2022, a steeper fall than in any other field. The Center for an Urban Future found roles with the highest AI exposure lost 29% of their entry-level postings while roles with minimal exposure gained 21%, and postings requiring AI skills grew 20%. For computer science and data students, the dataset that measures the collapse also shows where the demand went, so one documented AI project beats another round of applications. (The City Reporter | Center for an Urban Future)

Meta is asking engineers whether they want to become managers again, a year after cutting about 8,000 jobs and scrapping 6,000 open roles to flatten the company. Fortune reported that the Applied AI division is putting the question to individual contributors, even as headcount fell 3% quarter over quarter to 75,472. The coordination layer AI was supposed to remove has come back. If you are targeting large tech employers, the reopening roles reward organizing other people's work around an AI system. (Fortune)

Healthcare

California's legislature passed two bills barring AI from replacing a licensed professional's clinical judgment, and the governor has until September 30 to act. AB 2575 also protects health workers who override an AI output from retaliation. The same week, Dhruv Khullar argued in a New England Journal of Medicine perspective that AI is more likely to grow the clinical workforce than shrink it, because cheaper cataract surgery and joint replacement raised volumes and employment. Healthcare's training ladder has a legal floor under it that law's and engineering's do not. For pre-meds and nursing students, finishing licensure remains the highest-leverage move available. (National Nurses United | NEJM)

Ambient AI scribes let emergency physicians at Brigham and Women's Hospital document faster without moving a single patient through the department any quicker. Christopher Baugh, an emergency physician there, described 152 patients waiting against 61 acute care rooms and said the scribes have not changed the status quo. Oracle Health shipped a nurse-facing AI agent the same week into a workforce where only 41% of nurses use AI regularly against 57% of physicians. Scribing is one of the few paid apprenticeships in medicine, and the typing is the part being automated. If you use scribe work for clinical hours, pitch yourself on chart review rather than on speed. (STAT News | Healthcare Dive)
