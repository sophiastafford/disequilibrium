Keeping up with AI & the Entry-Level Job Market | September 8, 2026

What Changed This Week

If you apply to UBS for a 2027 graduate seat, the interview will ask how you actually use AI. Name a real task, what you handed a model, and what came back better. UBS is among the first large banks to write that requirement down. AI is moving out of the layoff statistics and into the fine print, meaning the interview rubric, the union contract and the state law.

A layoff number tells you what already happened to someone else. A written rule tells you what will be asked of you, in advance. The statistics this week moved the other way. Challenger, Gray & Christmas, an outplacement firm, found AI cited in only 3,462 August job cuts, its lowest monthly total since December 2025. The Economist reported that the gap between unemployment among 20-to-24-year-olds and the overall rate sits near a multi-decade low.

The rest of this issue is about where the fine print got written this week, in hiring criteria, a nurses’ contract and a fee agreement. You can read it before you sign anything.

Finance & Consulting

UBS made demonstrated AI ability an entry criterion for its 2027 graduate and intern class in Global Banking and Markets. Interviews now ask how candidates use the tools, and those hired enter an internal AI Fluency Pathway. OpenAI began rolling out GPT-6 Astra the same week, able to generate documents, spreadsheets and decks on a firm’s own templates. That is the first-year analyst deliverable. For finance recruits, start logging tasks where you can explain what you fed a model and how you checked it. (Financial Times | CNBC)

Santander and Coursera released 50,000 more free places on AI and data courses, bringing the program to 100,000, with no degree required. Demand moved the same week on campus, where a Michigan State course in computational and AI literacy is now full after failing to launch twice in 2025 for lack of enrollment. For students who need evidence for the interview UBS now runs, this is the cheapest way to get it. (Banco Santander | The State News)

Balyasny launched an AI Fellowship Program in New York paying up to $225,000 that rotates hires onto trading desks. Fellows start with the central AI team, then run three-month rotations as the dedicated AI partner to a portfolio manager. For technical students, this is a front-office path that skips the shrinking analyst class. (eFinancialCareers)

Healthcare

More than 25,000 Kaiser Permanente nurses picketed 22 California hospitals on September 1, demanding contract limits on AI. They want a formal role in deciding how AI tools reach patient care. Days earlier, the state legislature passed 16 AI bills, now on Governor Newsom’s desk. One of them, sponsored by the same union, would bar AI from independently performing any clinical function that requires a licensed professional. If you are entering a licensed field, read the AI language in the contract before you sign. (National Nurses United | Bloomberg Law)

Twelve utilization-review nurses at Montefiore Medical Center were terminated in July, their work absorbed by software from Datavant. The New York State Nurses Association calls it one of the first documented cases of US health professionals displaced by AI, and has filed a grievance. Those nurses had won a contract containing automation protections months earlier, after a 41-day strike. For nursing students, the exposed work here is chart review and insurer negotiation, not bedside care. (NJTODAY.NET)

Becker’s Hospital Review ranked transcriptionists, coders and patient-access representatives as the health roles most exposed to AI, and projected health information technologists and registrars to grow 16% by 2035. The same week, OpenAI connected ChatGPT Health to Epic, the records system covering more than 325 million patients, for pre-visit review and handoff summaries. For health informatics majors, the growth is on the side of the degree that builds and audits systems, not the side that processes records. (Becker's Hospital Review | TechCrunch)

Law

Legora ran OpenAI’s new Astra model on financial statement tie-out and its agent checked 41 documents in one run in minutes, catching all four errors Legora had planted, including a £500,000 gap hidden in a revenue note. Harvey said that Astra approaches legal work the way a discerning lawyer does. Multi-document cross-checking in diligence is a junior’s classic first real assignment. For law students, build the pitch around reviewing and defending agent output rather than producing the first draft. (Artificial Lawyer)

Above the Law argued that firms get no return on client-facing legal AI, because it cuts billable hours and billable hours are the revenue. By that logic the only clean win is back-office automation, which is why firms champion AI publicly and move slowly on billable work. Kyra Law, a UK firm built around AI from the start, launched the same week publishing fixed fees far below traditional rates. For 1Ls, a firm’s fee structure predicts its AI adoption better than its press releases. (Above the Law | Artificial Lawyer)

A LexisNexis survey found that 15% of lawyers at large UK firms now describe themselves as dependent on AI to do their job. The top three uses are drafting, legal research and document review, which is the first-year associate’s day. Vendors are also shifting the pitch from a generic junior associate to a doppelganger calibrated on one lawyer’s writing. For summer associates, get a real partner to mark up your work, because the tool cannot manufacture that. (Artificial Lawyer | Above the Law)
